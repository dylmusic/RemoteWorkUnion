Why Your Resume Matters for Remote Work

Remote jobs can get a lot of applicants. A strong remote work resume helps hiring platforms, recruiters, and applicant tracking systems quickly understand what you can do. Your remote work resume should be easy to scan, keyword-friendly, and focused on skills that show you can work independently online.

This is especially important for AI training, AI research, AI evaluation, data annotation, quality assurance, writing, research, operations, customer support, marketing, finance, and other knowledge-work roles. The goal is not to make your resume sound fancy. The goal is to make your experience easier to understand.

What Remote Work Platforms Look For

Whether you're applying to Mercor, Outlier AI, Handshake AI, or any remote job board, these are the things that matter most.

🎯

Relevant Skills

Can you do the work? List the tools, knowledge, and abilities that match the role you want. Be specific.

📋

Clear Experience

Where have you worked and what did you actually do? Even non-remote experience counts — describe it clearly.

💻

Remote-Ready Tools

Show you know how to work online: Google Workspace, Slack, Zoom, Notion, Airtable, and similar tools.

🔍

Research and Review Ability

Many AI and knowledge-work roles require careful reading, sound judgment, and clear written analysis.

🤖

ATS-Friendly Formatting

Plain text, standard headers, no graphics. Applicant tracking systems scan your resume before humans do.

✅

Truthful Keywords

Only include skills and tools you can actually explain. Keyword stuffing backfires quickly in interviews.

Your Skills Section Should Work Like a Keyword Bank

The skills section of your resume is one of the most important for remote and AI jobs. Applicant tracking systems search for specific words. Recruiters scan it in seconds. Make it clean, organized, and keyword-rich.

Example Skills Section Remote Work Asynchronous communication · Self-directed work · Time management · Online research · Written communication AI and Data AI training · Data annotation · Data labeling · Content rating · Response ranking · Quality assurance Software and Tools Google Workspace · Google Docs · Google Sheets · Slack · Zoom · Notion · Microsoft Office Domain Expertise Research · Writing · Editing · Analysis · Summarization · Fact-checking

Only include skills you can honestly explain or use.

Write a Clear Professional Summary

Your professional summary sits at the top of your resume and is usually the first thing a recruiter reads. Keep it to 2–3 sentences. Focus on what you do, your key skills, and the type of role you are looking for.

The Formula

[Job title or role type] with [X years / background in Y]. Skilled in [2–3 key skills]. Looking for remote opportunities in [area] where I can [contribute / apply / grow].

Example

"Detail-oriented researcher and writer with a background in education and content review. Skilled in online research, written communication, and data organization. Seeking remote AI training and data annotation roles where I can apply my analytical skills and attention to detail."

This approach works whether you have years of experience or you are just starting out. The key is to be specific, honest, and clear about what you are looking for.

Make Your Bullet Points More Useful

Bullet points under each job should describe what you actually did, not just your job title. Use action verbs, be specific where you can, and focus on outcomes or scale.

Before — Too Vague

  • Worked on customer support for a company
  • Helped with data entry tasks
  • Did research for marketing team
  • Responsible for writing reports
  • Assisted with various tasks

After — Specific and Strong

  • Resolved 40+ customer inquiries per week via email and chat, maintaining 95% satisfaction
  • Processed and validated 500+ records weekly in Google Sheets with 99% accuracy
  • Researched and summarized competitor data for marketing campaigns, improving team output speed
  • Wrote weekly performance reports reviewed by department managers and shared across teams
  • Coordinated scheduling, documentation, and logistics for a 12-person remote team

You don't always need numbers, but specificity always helps. "Resolved customer inquiries" is stronger than "helped customers." Add any measurable detail you honestly know.

Keep the Format Simple

For ATS-friendly resumes and remote job applications, simpler is almost always better. Avoid anything that makes it harder for software — or a busy recruiter — to read your resume quickly.

Use

  • Standard section headers (Summary, Skills, Experience, Education)
  • Clean single-column layout
  • Consistent font (Inter, Arial, Calibri, Georgia)
  • Bullet points for job duties
  • .docx or clean .pdf file format
  • Keywords from the job description
  • Dates in a consistent format (Month Year)

Avoid

  • Tables, columns, or multi-column layouts
  • Graphics, icons, or profile photos
  • Headers and footers with key information
  • Fancy fonts or colored text throughout
  • Text boxes that ATS systems can't parse
  • Keyword stuffing or skills you can't explain
  • Outdated formats like "Objective Statement"
