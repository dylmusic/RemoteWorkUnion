Most of the work is reviewing and improving AI. Typical tasks: comparing two model answers and picking the better one, rating responses against a rubric, rewriting weak answers, writing prompts that test the model, fact-checking, and flagging unsafe or low-quality output. Higher-value projects ask experts to review answers in their own field. The common thread is judgment — explaining clearly why something is right, wrong, or could be better.
Rates run from around $20/hr for entry-level annotation and rating up to $200+/hr for specialized expert review, with most generalist evaluation and prompt work in between. Data annotation is labeling raw data — tagging objects in images, transcribing audio, classifying text. AI training is broader: it includes annotation but also rating model outputs, writing prompts to test the AI, expert review, and RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) — the judgment work that directly shapes how a model behaves. Annotation tends to be more entry-level; training and evaluation reward expertise.
